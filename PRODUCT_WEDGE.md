# Product Wedge

Project Bid Bot should start with municipal service and construction contractors who repeatedly bid on similar scopes.

This is narrower than "all government procurement" and broader than one awkward local trade niche. The initial customer should already understand public bids, already lose estimator time to weak opportunities, and already have repeatable scope, pricing, and compliance patterns.

## Beachhead ICP

Target contractors:

- sell to municipalities, cities, school boards, utilities, transit agencies, or public housing authorities;
- bid at least monthly, ideally weekly;
- work on repeatable service or construction scopes;
- have enough historical market data to compare similar awards;
- are too small for a full capture/proposal department;
- feel estimator or owner time as the bottleneck.

Best first segments:

- road repair, asphalt, pavement markings, curb and sidewalk work;
- parks, landscaping, trails, playgrounds, fencing, arborist work;
- snow removal, salting, sweeping, drainage, seasonal maintenance;
- facilities maintenance, small renovations, roofing, signage, painting;
- civil works, site servicing, catch basins, water/sewer service connections;
- public-realm and small infrastructure subcontractors.

Best first geography:

- one metro or province/state where public procurement records and award history are accessible;
- Toronto/GTA is fine for the prototype, but customer discovery should not be limited to Ontario if stronger buyers appear elsewhere.

## Sharp Pain

The buyer does not wake up wanting an AI procurement dashboard. They want fewer wasted bids.

The product promise:

> We tell you which public bids are worth your time, what price range is realistic, and what would make you non-compliant before you spend 20 hours on the package.

The emotional pain:

- owners chase noisy bid portals after hours;
- estimators spend time on work they should have skipped;
- teams miss addenda, mandatory site meetings, bonding, insurance, certifications, or document requirements;
- pricing is guessed from gut feel instead of comparable awards;
- no one has a clean memory of which buyers and scopes they should pursue.

## What We Are Not Building First

Avoid these traps:

- a generic federal GovCon capture platform;
- another search dashboard with AI summaries;
- automated submission without human approval;
- one-off proposal writing for every industry;
- compliance claims without checking actual solicitation documents;
- a CRM before the bid/no-bid workflow is painful and trusted.

## Killer Workflow

The first product loop should be:

1. Ingest a contractor profile, capabilities, service area, capacity, certifications, insurance, bonding, and past jobs.
2. Watch target bid sources for new opportunities.
3. Show only the small weekly queue worth attention.
4. For each bid, explain pursue/review/monitor/skip with hard blockers and soft risks.
5. Estimate a realistic bid range from similar awards.
6. Extract compliance requirements from the solicitation package.
7. Produce an owner-review packet: checklist, likely bid range, missing docs, questions for buyer, and recommended next action.

The customer should feel like the product is doing capture ops, not showing a prettier portal.

## Validation Questions

Ask target customers:

- How many public bids did you review last month?
- How many did you submit?
- How many did you later regret spending time on?
- Who decides bid/no-bid today?
- What documents or requirements most often kill a bid late?
- How do you price against similar public awards now?
- What would you pay to save 10-20 estimator hours per month?
- Would you trust an approval packet if it cited the exact solicitation clauses and similar awards?

## Success Metric

The first milestone is not "more bids found."

The first milestone is:

- 10 real contractor conversations;
- 3 contractors willing to send past bid documents or recent opportunities;
- 1 contractor willing to run a weekly bid queue through the prototype;
- measurable avoided bid hours or a real submitted bid packet prepared from the workflow.
